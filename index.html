The Problem

Punjab is a major agricultural state of India, and its farming relies heavily on groundwater. The rice-wheat cropping system needs a lot of irrigation, much of it pumped from underground. When more water is pumped out than is naturally replenished, groundwater levels fall, which threatens long-term farming and drinking-water security. Academic research shows several factors interact here: crop choice, climate, technology, electricity policy and economic incentives. Government policy is one part of that picture, not the sole cause.
